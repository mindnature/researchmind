# Universal Scholar Source Discovery

This protocol is used when the user supplies only a scholar name or an incomplete source set.

## 1. Identity first

Before collecting research material, resolve:

- canonical name and common aliases;
- discipline and main institutions;
- active years;
- ORCID / institutional profile / prize profile when available;
- similarly named scholars who could contaminate the corpus.

Ask the user only when two plausible identities remain and the choice materially changes the distillation.

## 2. Build a source map before bulk reading

Search in layers rather than collecting indiscriminately.

### Layer A — contemporaneous process evidence

Search combinations of the scholar name with:

- archive / papers / special collections
- research notebook / laboratory notebook / field notes
- manuscript / draft / working notes
- correspondence / letters
- research report / project report
- oral history collection metadata that points to primary objects

Prefer institutional archives, libraries, museums, university special collections and official personal archives.

### Layer B — formal research outputs

Find:

- seminal papers and major negative/failed lines when published;
- preprints and technical reports;
- supplements, protocols, data/code repositories;
- patents where relevant;
- correction/retraction/reply literature when relevant.

Use original publisher/repository records where possible.

### Layer C — first-person retrospective evidence

Find:

- Nobel/Turing/major-prize lectures;
- long-form interviews;
- oral histories;
- autobiographies/memoirs;
- retrospective talks about discoveries and failures.

Record the retrospective date separately from the historical decision date.

### Layer D — third-party historical reconstruction

Find:

- biographies;
- history-of-science / intellectual-history scholarship;
- institutional documentary histories;
- collaborator and student recollections;
- well-sourced obituaries or profile essays when useful.

Use these to discover primary objects and alternative interpretations, not to silently substitute for process evidence.

## 3. Search for failures, reversals and abandoned lines explicitly

Do not let citation rankings produce a success-only corpus. Search terms should include where appropriate:

- failed theory / failed model / wrong prediction
- abandoned project / discontinued line
- controversy / dispute / criticism / response
- correction / retraction / reversal
- negative result / anomaly
- changed mind / revised view

The goal is not scandal collection. The goal is to identify boundary conditions for research heuristics.

## 4. Search by Episode, not only by person

Once major candidate Episodes are identified, perform a second search centered on each event:

`<scholar> + <event> + notebook/letter/draft/interview/history`

This often surfaces process evidence that broad author searches miss.

## 5. Team attribution search

For each major Episode, identify collaborators and search them separately. A famous scholar's archive may omit the experimental decision that is documented in a student's dissertation, technician's notebook, collaborator's letter, or lab report.

## 6. Source availability ceiling

After reconnaissance, classify the scholar:

- `publication_only`
- `public_retrospective`
- `process_evidence`
- `golden_archive`

Do not claim a deeper reconstruction than the available evidence supports.

## 7. Minimal source counts by depth

These are targets, not quotas that justify low-quality sources.

### quick

- 5–10 high-quality sources
- at least 1 B source per major Episode
- use C/D only to locate process questions

### standard

- 20–40 key sources
- mix of B/C/D, plus A wherever publicly available
- at least 5 candidate Episodes
- deliberately search for counter/failure Episodes

### deep

- systematic archive mapping
- direct primary-object inspection for the most important Episodes
- team-source triangulation

### golden

- exact stable locators for decisive evidence
- contemporaneous process evidence for central Episodes
- explicit competing historical interpretations
- audit trail sufficient for external review

## 8. Copyright and access discipline

Use public or user-provided material through normal access paths. Do not bypass paywalls, private archives, authentication controls, DRM or platform restrictions. Store metadata, short necessary quotations and locators unless the user has rights to retain full texts locally.

## 9. Discovery completion report

Before synthesis, report:

- identity confidence;
- source count by A/B/C/D;
- process-evidence availability;
- major candidate Episodes;
- likely counter/failure Episodes;
- unresolved archive blockers;
- source availability ceiling.
