# Distillation Grade

ResearchMind separates **requested depth** from **evidence-supported grade**.

`depth` is what the user asks the system to attempt. `distillation_grade` is what the available evidence actually supports.

## Grade A — `A_archival`

Archival deep-dive. Core Episodes have contemporaneous process evidence such as notebooks, drafts, correspondence, lab records, or other directly inspectable research-process objects. Team attribution and micro-decisions can be reconstructed with relatively high confidence.

## Grade B — `B_process_informed`

Process-informed. Some contemporaneous process evidence exists and materially constrains reconstruction, but decisive Episodes still depend partly on formal publications, retrospective testimony, or historical synthesis.

## Grade C — `C_retrospective`

Retrospective. Evidence is dominated by publications plus first-person lectures, interviews, oral histories, or memoir-like accounts. Research-program trajectories can be reconstructed better than moment-to-moment decisions. Micro-decision claims must be lowered in confidence.

## Grade D — `D_publication_based`

Publication-based. The corpus is mainly formal outputs and public metadata. Research themes, method choices visible in papers, and publication trajectories may be analyzed, but private decision processes, rejected alternatives, failure handling, and tacit judgment cannot be claimed with high confidence.

## Evidence profile

Each scholar profile records:

- contemporaneous process coverage;
- publication coverage;
- first-person retrospective coverage;
- third-party dependency;
- micro-decision reconstruction confidence;
- research-program reconstruction confidence.

Do not infer grade from fame, award status, seniority, or whether the scholar is living. Grade is evidence-dependent.

## User-facing disclosure

Every generated Scholar Advisor must display its grade and source-availability ceiling before giving scholar-specific advice.
