# Methodology

## Unit of analysis: Research Decision Episode

The minimum useful unit is not a paper or a quote. It is a bounded decision episode:

`state of knowledge → trigger/anomaly → alternatives → action → evidence update → result`

A single publication may contain multiple episodes; one episode may span notebooks, letters, papers, and later oral histories.

## Distillation sequence

1. Register sources before interpretation.
2. Segment evidence with stable locators.
3. Build a chronology.
4. Freeze `known_at_the_time` before reading the outcome when possible.
5. Extract candidate episodes.
6. Attribute actions to people/teams.
7. Generate candidate heuristics using operational verbs.
8. Search for a counter-episode or failure mode.
9. Promote only heuristics whose boundaries survive contrastive review.
10. Test on held-out historical and new research problems.

## Three tests for a useful heuristic

A candidate heuristic should have:

- recurrence: the decision pattern appears beyond one isolated anecdote, or has a clearly informative success/failure pair;
- generativity: it produces a concrete action or discriminating question on a new case;
- boundary specificity: it states when the rule should be weakened or rejected.

## Anti-patterns

Reject outputs such as:

- "be curious"
- "think independently"
- "follow the evidence"
- "use first principles"

unless the agent can convert them into observable decision operations backed by episodes.

## Counterfactual discipline

Do not claim that a heuristic caused a scientific success merely because it was present. Phrase causal claims conservatively unless the historical evidence supports the causal link.
